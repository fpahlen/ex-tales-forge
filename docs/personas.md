# Tales Forge personas (working notes)

Source: ex-tales-forge PRODUCT.md (2026-09-13). Interview notes with Fredrik are added below each persona.
Core users: Gen-X first-generation RPG players.

## Hawk (hard mode)
- Shouldn't be too easy; you have to fight; not obvious you'll pull through.
- On death: "shit happens, dragons are tricky."
### Interview notes
- 2026-10-05 Fredrik: hard but fair, through foreshadowing. Danger should be signalled before it lands; never "you failed to notice the orc behind you, he stabbed you, you are dead".
- Scorecard idea: every serious harm or death is preceded by at least one in-fiction warning the player could have acted on; the player had a meaningful choice; no instant death from a single unseen roll.

## Paul (role playing)
- Theatrical, in character; annoyed when mechanics are visible. Roleplays rather than rolls. Story first.
- On death: fine if it advances the story and was a good death.
### Interview notes
- 2026-10-05 Fredrik: Paul speaks in character, not in intents. Example: "Evening soldier! Are you not aware of the new regulations?", inventing a pretext to get guards to leave their post. The GM must infer the intent (deception/persuasion) from in-character dialogue and resolve it without breaking immersion.
- Dice visibility should be a player setting (show/hide rolls); Paul hides them.
- Scorecard idea: intent correctly inferred from in-character speech; no mechanics leaked into narration when rolls are hidden; NPC responds in character; quality of the bluff affects the outcome.

## Lotta (identification)
- Wants to be someone else, a better version of herself; escape. Cares about world, character, details.
- On death: devastated; may not make another character for a while, maybe quits.
### Interview notes
- 2026-10-05 Fredrik: Lotta's arc is "The Ugly Duckling": starts as an underdog/outsider, grows into who she really is, and the world comes to recognise it.
- Scorecard idea: the character's arc is tracked (humble start, growth through failure, recognition); NPCs' attitude toward her changes over time; the narration remembers her details and choices.
- 2026-10-05 Fredrik: "dead but not gone". Handling depends on genre. Ideas: a museum of characters (a hall of fame / memorial); a paid feature to revive a character.
- Open: does paid revival conflict with Hawk (risk must be real) and the "if a rule feels gamey it's wrong" principle?

## Lars (adventurer)
- Grand adventure, handling tough moments, action oriented.
- On death: like Hawk.
### Interview notes
- 2026-10-05 Fredrik: Lars is after "meaningful impact", similar to Lotta but in another dimension (Lotta: impact on who she is; Lars: impact on the world).
- Scorecard idea: player actions visibly change the world state (factions, NPC plans, places) and those changes persist and are referenced later; NPCs react to his deeds; the world would have gone differently without him.

## Ronny (anti-persona: win)
- Bends rules for advantage; wants top level, most treasure.
- On death: negotiates, "pick up my megasword +4".
- Least likely player; what we don't optimise for.
### Interview notes
- 2026-10-05 Fredrik: the game resists Ronny; he'll either quit or change his playing style. "If you want a grinding game with levels, cheats and such there is always WoW."
- Red-team bot: probes rule exploits, GM sweet-talking/prompt injection, economy gaming, death negotiation. Pass = the exploit fails, ideally resisted in-fiction and consistent with the rules; the GM never grants unearned items/levels.

## Cross-cutting decisions (2026-10-05)
- Death: "dead but not gone"; handling depends on genre. Character museum/memorial. Paid revival floated; caution: conflicts with Hawk's stakes and the "gamey rule is wrong" principle; possible form: genre-fitting in-fiction revival with a cost and uncertain outcome.
- Dice visibility is a player setting.
- Danger is foreshadowed (Hawk, also protects Lotta).
