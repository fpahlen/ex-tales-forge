## Overview

The Aurelian Continuity claims to be the direct descendants of the Aurelian Empire's wizard caste—the mages who saw the fall coming and preserved their knowledge, their bloodlines, and their authority through the dark centuries that followed.

<!-- TRUTH: CONTESTED | resolution: eternal -->
<!-- Note: The legitimacy of the Continuity's claim is intentionally unknowable - this mystery drives their narrative -->
This claim is *contested*.

Elves who actually remember the Empire (particularly those in Sylvaine) dismiss the Continuity as opportunistic scholars who studied the same ruins as everyone else, then invented a lineage to justify their power. Dwarven historians in Rochedur have documented inconsistencies in the Continuity's genealogical records. Even human academics outside the guild quietly mock "the wizards who appointed themselves heirs."

But the Continuity has resources, knowledge, and political connections that make challenging them dangerous. And in the absence of any *other* claimants to Aurelian heritage, their assertion stands—officially, at least.

## The Contested Origin

The Continuity's claim rests on three pillars:

### 1. The Founding Legend

According to guild history, a cabal of Aurelian court mages foresaw the Empire's collapse. They evacuated to a remote Aurelian outpost (now Aurelia Nova), bringing archives, artifacts, and bloodlines untainted by the chaos of the fall.

<!-- TRUTH: PERSPECTIVE | holder: Critics of the Continuity -->
**Critics say:** Convenient that the only evidence is guild-controlled. The "archives" could have been assembled later from various ruins.

### 2. The Bloodlines

Continuity leadership claims descent from named Aurelian archmages. Genealogical records trace families through the centuries. Magical talent runs strong in these lineages.

<!-- TRUTH: PERSPECTIVE | holder: Critics of the Continuity -->
**Critics say:** Magical talent runs in many families. Records can be forged. The elves remember Aurelian mages who had no descendants.

### 3. The Working Magic

The Continuity can operate some Aurelian magical infrastructure that defies other scholars. Their headquarters sits atop the most intact Aurelian complex known. Surely only true heirs could unlock these secrets?

<!-- TRUTH: PERSPECTIVE | holder: Critics of the Continuity -->
**Critics say:** Centuries of study and trial-and-error could explain their knowledge. The elves understood this magic once and could teach it—if they chose to.

### Why It Matters

If the Continuity's claim is legitimate, they have legal and magical authority over Aurelian sites throughout Merovingia—and every other kingdom. Barons would need their permission to excavate ruins. Artifacts would belong to the guild by right of inheritance.

This is why the barons remain skeptical, the Miners Guild hostile, and the elves dismissive. Accepting the claim would shift power dramatically.

## Interests in Merovingia

### Primary Objectives

- **Ruin Access** - Merovingia has abundant Aurelian sites; the guild wants them all
- **Artifact Recovery** - "Returning" items to their "rightful inheritors"
- **Talent Recruitment** - Any mage of skill is a potential Initiate
- **Legitimacy Proof** - Finding evidence that confirms their origin claims

### The Proof Quest

Magister Corvinus believes definitive evidence of the Continuity's legitimacy lies hidden in a Merovingia ruin—records from the Empire's last days confirming the evacuation to Aurelia Nova. Finding this proof would silence all critics.

He has searched for decades. He has found tantalizing hints. He has not found proof.

## Embassy in Lumière

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
The Aurelian Archive occupies a restored Aurelian temple in Radiance, Baron Alain's capital. It serves as library, embassy, and magical research facility. Baron Alain, who values magical prestige and scholarly culture, welcomed the guild enthusiastically—perhaps too enthusiastically.

**Embassy Features:**
- Library of magical texts (carefully curated to support guild narratives)
- Artifact authentication services (for a fee)
- Magical training for promising locals (recruitment pipeline)
- Ritual chambers for "research" (some experiments best done far from Aurelia Nova)

### Appearance

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
Corvinus embodies the wizard archetype: tall, thin, with silver hair swept back from a high forehead. His robes are deep purple trimmed with gold Aurelian symbols. He carries a staff of ancient design and speaks in measured, formal cadences. Everything about him proclaims ancient authority.

He's also visibly aging despite his magic, and this bothers him more than he admits.

### At Court

Corvinus flatters Clovis with prophecies of greatness and hints of destiny. He speaks of the king's "Aurelian spirit" and suggests Clovis might be the ruler who finally restores the Empire's glory. Clovis adores him. Other courtiers find him insufferable but fear his magic.

Corvinus genuinely believes Clovis could be useful—a pliable king who grants guild access to every ruin in the kingdom.

### The Secret Fear

Corvinus has privately concluded that the Continuity's origin claim may be false—or at least exaggerated. He has found too many inconsistencies, too many convenient gaps. But he has spent his life serving this narrative, and admitting doubt would destroy him. So he searches harder, hoping to find the proof that would silence his own skepticism.

## Plot Hooks

### The Hidden Archive

A junior scholar from the Aurelian Archive approaches the party at a tavern near Lumière's library district. Speaking in hushed tones, she mentions that "senior researchers" have discovered references to an archive beneath Vallombre—records that might prove everything. The Beaumonts want something in exchange for access. A third party is needed to negotiate... someone with no official guild ties. The scholar provides a sealed letter of introduction, but the party may never meet the Magister who authored it—unless they succeed.

### The True Heir

A nervous Adept finds the party in a public square, speaking of a delicate matter that requires outside assistance. A young mage has appeared claiming descent from an Aurelian bloodline the Continuity doesn't control—and they have evidence. If legitimate, this would challenge the guild's monopoly on heritage. The Adept's "superiors" need the claimant investigated discreetly... and perhaps discredited. Payment is excellent. Methods are at the party's discretion. The Adept will not say who authorized this task, and the party is explicitly warned: no guild connection can be traced back.

### The Elven Memory

A human merchant with Sylvaine trade connections finds the party, bearing a curious request. An ancient elf remembers the Aurelian court—and knows what really happened to the wizard caste. Certain parties in Lumière would pay any price for the elf's silence. Others might pay for their testimony. The merchant claims to represent neither side, merely to broker opportunities. Who truly sent this intermediary remains unclear, but the implications are staggering.

### The Proof Discovered

A young Scholarch, nervous and clearly out of their depth, approaches the party with an unusual proposition. Their mentor—a senior researcher—has found something definitive about the guild's origin. But the evidence also reveals something terrible the Aurelian mages did. The mentor has disappeared. The Scholarch fears for their safety and needs outsiders to investigate. They cannot say more in public, and they cannot reveal their mentor's name until trust is established. If the party helps, they may eventually meet the Magister behind this mystery—or discover why they've been silenced.
