# The Great Guilds

*Three powers older than kingdoms, reaching from distant capitals to shape Merovingia's fate*

## Overview

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
While the twelve barons squabble over Aurelian ruins and King Clovis plays at ruling, three ancient guilds watch from beyond Merovingia's borders. Each maintains a formal embassy at court, ambassadors who bow to the crown while advancing agendas that dwarf any single kingdom's concerns.

The guilds are not merely traders or craftsmen—they are transnational powers with resources rivaling nations, networks spanning continents, and memories longer than dynasties.

## The Triumvirate Compact

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
An ancient treaty—signed before Merovingia existed as a kingdom—binds the three guilds to a wary peace. The Compact forbids:

- Open warfare between guild members
- Assassination of guild leadership
- Interference with officially declared guild operations
- Destruction of guild property without arbitration

The Compact does *not* forbid:
- Economic competition
- Information warfare
- Recruitment of each other's members
- "Unofficial" operations with plausible deniability

Violations are adjudicated by a rotating tribunal of senior members from all three guilds—a process so slow and political that most disputes are resolved (or buried) long before judgment.

## Guild Dynamics

### The Uneasy Truce

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
The guilds maintain surface civility while constantly undermining each other:

- **Miners vs. Wizards**: Bitter disputes over Aurelian underground sites. The Miners want ore; the Wizards claim magical significance. Every major ruin sparks jurisdictional conflict.

- **Miners vs. Thieves**: The Grey Consortium skims from ore shipments; the Brethren hire security to stop them. A quiet war of theft and counter-theft.

- **Wizards vs. Thieves**: The Aurelias need artifacts "acquired" discreetly; the Consortium charges premium rates. Transactional but tense—the wizards resent paying, the thieves resent being treated as servants.

### At King Clovis's Court

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
All three ambassadors maintain formal relations with the crown:

- They attend royal functions and participate in court ceremonies
- They offer "gifts" to the king in exchange for favorable treatment
- They carefully avoid openly contradicting each other in public
- They ruthlessly compete for royal favor in private

<!-- TRUTH: PERSPECTIVE | holder: King Clovis IV -->
Clovis finds the ambassadors useful for playing against the barons—foreign powers who owe him nothing but flattery, unlike barons who might claim his throne.

## Why Merovingia Matters

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
Each guild has specific interests in the kingdom:

**Miners Guild**
- Ferroigne's iron deposits
- Rochedur's stone quarries
- Glacière's rare minerals
- Aurelian underground complexes

**Thieves Guild**
- Vallombre's information markets
- Montfort's trade routes
- Aquilon's port access
- Artifacts from every barony

**Ancient Aurelias**
- Aurelian ruins (which Merovingia has in abundance)
- Magical artifacts requiring "authentication"
- Recruiting talented mages
- Proving their contested origin claims

## Foreign Cities

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
The guild headquarters lie beyond Merovingia's borders in lands considered more "civilized":

### Irondeep

A dwarven-human city carved into the southern mountains. The Iron Council—comprised of guild masters and mine lords—rules by consensus. Known for master smithing, banking, and serving as neutral ground for treaties between hostile powers.

### Velantis

A cosmopolitan port on the western coast where laws are suggestions and cargo manifests are creative fiction. The Prince of Velantis asks no questions about what passes through his harbor, and his cut of all trade makes him fabulously wealthy. The Grey Consortium's headquarters occupies an entire district.

### Aurelia Nova

Built atop the most intact Aurelian complex known to exist, this eastern city is dominated by magical academies and artifact markets. The Continuity Council—a wizard conclave—rules in theory, though internal politics are labyrinthine. Scholars from across the world come to study here, lending credibility to the guild's claims of Aurelian descent.

## Plot Hooks

<!-- Note: Plot hooks are GM tools, not canonical truths -->

### The Compact Violation

One guild has secretly broken the Triumvirate Compact—assassinating a member of another guild and framing a Merovingian baron. The party might be hired to investigate, or caught in the crossfire as guild agents descend on the kingdom.

### The Fourth Chair

<!-- TRUTH: CONTESTED | resolution: eternal -->
Rumors speak of a fourth guild that once sat at the Triumvirate table—the Navigators, masters of sea and sky. What happened to them? Did they die out, or merely go into hiding? Someone is leaving their symbols in Merovingia.
<!-- Note: The truth about the Navigators is intentionally unknowable - the mystery IS the point -->

### The Ambassador's Ball

All three ambassadors will attend the King's Winter Ball. Each wants the party to perform a task during the festivities. Accepting all three jobs might prove... complicated.

### Jurisdictional Nightmare

An Aurelian ruin has been discovered that contains ore deposits, magical artifacts, AND documents the Thieves Guild wants to disappear. All three guilds claim priority. The barons want them all gone. The party is hired to "resolve" the situation.
