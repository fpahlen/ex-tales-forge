## Physical Appearance

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
Clovis is handsome in a soft, untested way—pale from hours spent indoors, with delicate features that would be called beautiful on a woman. His golden hair is always immaculately styled, his clothes the finest in the realm, yet there's something hollow about his bearing. The Sacred Crown of Merovingia sits awkwardly on his head, and he unconsciously adjusts it constantly. His eyes dart nervously in large gatherings, though he affects an air of bored superiority.

## Personality

### The Mask
<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
<!-- Note: This describes how Clovis presents himself - observable behavior -->
To the court, Clovis presents himself as a divine ruler, touched by the gods and destined for greatness. He speaks of his "vision for Merovingia" and drops hints about grand reforms and military campaigns that never materialize. He demands elaborate ceremonies acknowledging his majesty and becomes sullen when not the center of attention.

### The Reality
<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
<!-- Note: This is the canonical true nature of Clovis, known to omniscient narrator -->
Beneath the posturing lies a deeply insecure young man who knows, on some level, that he's in over his head. He craves validation desperately but cannot distinguish genuine counsel from manipulation. He's not stupid—occasionally sharp insights slip through—but his emotional fragility and need for approval consistently override his judgment.

## Key Character Flaws

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
### 1. Easily Flattered
Clovis's deepest vulnerability. Anyone who praises his "wisdom" or compares him favorably to his legendary ancestor Clovis I can secure royal favor. Experienced courtiers have learned the precise ratio of flattery to suggestion that bypasses his critical thinking entirely.

### 2. Spite-Driven
Clovis remembers every slight, real or imagined. A baron who failed to bow deeply enough a year ago may find their trade routes suddenly taxed. This makes him predictable—and exploitable by those who can direct his petty vengeance toward their enemies.

### 3. Legacy Obsession
Haunted by comparison to Clovis I (the founder) and his own father, he desperately seeks ways to prove his divine right. He's easily drawn to quests for legendary artifacts, prophecies about his greatness, or anything that validates his rule. Several barons have already fabricated "ancient texts" for this purpose.

### 4. Paranoid Gullibility
A dangerous combination. Clovis suspects everyone of plotting against him—except the people who actually are. He trusts those who tell him what he wants to hear and distrusts honest advisors. This has led him to exile loyal servants while embracing sycophants.

## The Royal Court

### Lord Chancellor Malaric
<!-- TRUTH: CONTESTED | resolution: eternal -->
<!-- Note: Malaric's true nature is intentionally unknowable - the mystery IS the point -->
The true power behind the throne. An ancient man (some whisper he's served three kings, which should be impossible) who "merely advises" while making every real decision. He presents choices to Clovis in ways that inevitably lead to Malaric's preferred outcome. No one knows his true agenda—perhaps he simply craves power, or perhaps something darker motivates his eternal patience.

### Lady Isabeau (The Queen Mother)
<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
Clovis's widowed mother, bitter and calculating. She blames him for being "weak like his father" while simultaneously enabling his worst impulses. She focuses obsessively on arranging his marriage to consolidate power and genuinely believes she's protecting him by controlling him. She maintains her own network of spies independent of the court.

### Sir Percival the Bright
<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
Captain of the Royal Guard and the only person who genuinely believes in Clovis. His naive faith that the king will "grow into greatness" is both touching and tragic. Clovis keeps him close partly out of vanity (Percival's devotion flatters him) and partly because some small part of him wishes he could be the man Percival sees.

## Why the Barons Tolerate Him

<!-- TRUTH: ABSOLUTE -->
The twelve barons could likely depose Clovis tomorrow—yet none has moved against him. Why?

1. **Better the puppet you know.** A weak king is predictable. A succession war would be chaos.
2. **No clear successor.** Any baron who claimed the throne would face eleven enemies.
3. **The Beaumont ambiguity.** Garrick and Nyra Beaumont have the strongest claim after House Merovech, but they're playing a longer game.
4. **Divine legitimacy.** The Church still recognizes House Merovech. Moving against the king risks religious sanction.
5. **Mutual deterrence.** Any baron who acts will be destroyed by the others—not out of loyalty to Clovis, but to prevent a rival's ascension.

<!-- TRUTH: PERSPECTIVE | holder: Political Analysts -->
Clovis remains on the throne not because anyone believes in him, but because removing him is more dangerous than tolerating him.

## Adventure Hooks

<!-- Note: Adventure hooks are GM tools, not canonical truths - no tags needed -->

### The Forged Prophecy
A baron (perhaps Seraphine Lumis) has commissioned a "discovered" ancient prophecy declaring Clovis will reclaim a legendary artifact—one that happens to be in a rival's territory. The party might be hired to retrieve it, or to expose the forgery.

### The Royal Wedding
Clovis must marry. Every faction is maneuvering to provide the bride. The party could be hired by one faction—or tasked with protecting a candidate—or investigating a rival's "perfect bride" who seems too perfect.

### The Exile's Return
Someone Clovis banished in a fit of spite (perhaps a genuinely loyal advisor) has information critical to the realm. The party must find them—but bringing them back risks the king's wrath.

### The Chancellor's Secret
<!-- TRUTH: CONTESTED | resolution: eternal -->
What is Lord Chancellor Malaric's true nature? How has he served for so long? The party might stumble onto evidence of something deeply wrong at the heart of the court.

### The Assassination Attempt
Someone is genuinely trying to kill Clovis. The party must protect him—but the investigation reveals so many people *could* want him dead that identifying the true culprit is nearly impossible.

## Roleplaying Notes

<!-- Note: Roleplaying guidance for GMs, not canonical truths - no tags needed -->

When portraying Clovis:
- Speak with affected grandeur that occasionally cracks into whining
- React poorly to anyone who doesn't show sufficient deference
- Show occasional flashes of genuine insight or kindness, hinting at the king he could have been
- Always be adjusting his crown
- Reference "my vision" and "my legacy" frequently
- Be transparently manipulated while believing you're the cleverest person in the room
